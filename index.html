How a New York City public school teacher gets hired — and fired

Two flowcharts tracing the full-time kindergarten through grade 12 teaching job from state certification to a probationary appointment, and the separate paths by which that job can end. Each step is annotated with how long it takes, who is involved and the law it hinges on.

Hiring: from certification to a probationary appointment

Two lanes run in parallel. The state controls the license; the city school system runs the application, the school-level hiring and the appointment. A candidate generally must hold a New York State certificate, or be on track to earn one, before a city school can put them to work.

New York City uses school-based hiring. A principal interviews candidates, usually with a demonstration lesson, then recommends and nominates the chosen candidate — the action that triggers pre-employment processing. Already-appointed teachers move between schools through the Open Market Transfer System.10

TIMEDays to months; nomination expires after 60 days without fingerprinting typical
WHOPrincipal recommends; superintendent / Chancellor appoints

The new hire receives credentials and a salary placement. Starting pay as of September 14, 2025 was $68,902 with a bachelor's degree and $77,455 with a master's, with steps for prior experience and differentials for graduate credits.

State law sets a probationary period of four years and a day for a teacher appointed under a new license (three years for a teacher who already earned tenure elsewhere; as little as two years with prior substitute "Jarema" credit). Tenure can be granted on successful completion.

Flowchart 2

Firing: three distinct ways the job can end

How a teacher can be removed depends almost entirely on one fact — whether they have earned tenure. A probationary teacher can be let go with little process. A tenured teacher is entitled to a formal hearing under state law. Layoffs follow a separate, seniority-based track.

The pivotal question

Has the teacher earned tenure?

Track A · probationary (untenured)

Discontinuance of probationary service14

Service may be ended at any time during probation, by a vote of the board on the superintendent's recommendation. No cause hearing is required.

TIMEAny time during probation statutory
WHOSuperintendent recommends; board votes (in NYC, delegated to the Chancellor)
LAWEducation Law §2573(1)(a)

The legal bar is low15

A probationer can be let go for almost any reason, unless the teacher shows the termination was unconstitutional, violated a statute or was done in bad faith.

WHOCourt, only if the teacher sues to challenge it
LAWCourt-made "bad faith" standard; statutory base Education Law §2573

60-day written notice14

A teacher who will not be recommended for tenure must be notified in writing before probation ends, and may request a written statement of reasons.16

TIMEAt least 60 days before probation ends statutory
WHOSuperintendent
LAWEducation Law §2573(1); statement of reasons §3031

Limited review — advisory only17

The teacher may seek review before a three-member committee (one pick each by teacher and city, plus a third). Its recommendation is advisory; the Chancellor decides.

TIMEScheduled within ~1 year where joined with a license review rule
WHOTripartite committee (advisory); Chancellor decides

A separate license-termination action under Chancellor's Regulation C-31 can bar the person from any city school, not just this job.18

Separate path · not "for cause"

Layoffs and excessing — by seniority25

"Excessing" moves a teacher out of a school when positions shrink, but is not a loss of employment — pay continues. A true citywide layoff, when positions are abolished, follows "last in, first out": the least senior teacher in the license area goes first, with recall rights from a preferred eligible list.26 The Absent Teacher Reserve pool that once held excessed staff was wound down in 2021.27

TIMENo fixed clock; driven by budget and seniority order
WHOCity school system; ranked by citywide seniority
LAWEducation Law §2588 (NYC layoffs, recall list); excessing per DOE–UFT contract
Two more ways out, separate from firing: a teacher may resign or retire voluntarily, and the state education department can revoke a teaching certificate for cause under its moral-character rules (8 NYCRR Part 83) — which bars teaching anywhere in the state, not just the city.28

How this was built, and what to double-check

These two flowcharts compress a large body of law, regulation and contract into the actual sequence a teacher moves through. The aim is to be accurate about the order of steps, the timing, who acts and the governing law — not to capture every exception. Tenure law, the disciplinary statute and the union contract each carry edge cases that a real case would turn on.

Durations are labeled statutory or rule when fixed by law or regulation, and typical or official when they reflect observed or agency-stated practice with no guaranteed turnaround. On the tenured firing track, the day counts are statutory minimum steps; in practice a §3020-a case often runs well over a year. Sourcing priority was, in order: New York State statute and regulation; official state education department and New York City Public Schools sources; and the United Federation of Teachers contract and its explainers for city-specific terms.

Points to verify before relying on this for a specific case

  • solidThe big structural facts are well established: the standalone edTPA exam was dropped in 2022 for a program assessment; probation runs four years and a day; tenured discipline runs through §3020-a, with the ineffective-ratings track in the separate §3020-b; layoffs are seniority based under §2588.
  • checkHiring durations (exam scoring, application review, school hiring, fingerprint clearance) are estimates or agency-stated practice, not guaranteed timelines, and vary case to case.
  • checkAnnual application and transfer deadlines change every year and are deliberately not shown as fixed dates.
  • checkA few citations are best-available rather than confirmed verbatim: the exam-content regulation beyond §80-3.3, the precise current number of the Panel for Educational Policy bylaw on probationary review (renumbered over time), and the statutory peg for the 60-day nomination rule (a DOE administrative practice).
  • checkThe probationary "bad faith" standard is settled doctrine drawn from case law; a published legal claim should pin it to a named court decision.
  • checkHow New York City formally satisfies the §3020-a "board vote" under mayoral control, and the current status of the Absent Teacher Reserve in 2024 to 2026, were not confirmed to primary-source standard here.

  • Every step is cited, but laws, regulations and the union contract change — confirm against the live source before relying on a detail for a real case.
  • Timings labeled "typical" or "official" are estimates or stated practice, not guaranteed turnarounds; statutory day counts are minimums and real cases often run longer.
